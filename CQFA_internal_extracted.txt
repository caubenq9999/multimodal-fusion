ABSTRACT
Modality imbalance remains a fundamental challenge in multimodal learning,
where dominant modalities disproportionately shape optimization while weaker
modalities are underutilized. Existing studies mainly analyze this problem through
optimization behavior, training losses, and final task performance, paying compara-
tively little attention to the intrinsic structure of multimodal representations. In this
work, we revisit modality imbalance from a structural perspective and identify two
coupled failure modes: representation-level imbalance, in which modalities occupy
mismatched low-dimensional subspaces, and fusion-level imbalance, in which
shared fusion layers suppress weak-modality contributions through destructive
cross-modal entanglement. This decomposition suggests that correcting optimiza-
tion imbalance alone is insufficient, as improved representation alignment does
not guarantee balanced utilization after fusion. To address these two sources of
imbalance, we propose Cross-Modal Query Fusion and Alignment (CQFA), a
query-guided framework that explicitly separates modality-specific representa-
tion learning from controlled cross-modal interaction through staged training and
query-partitioned fusion. Specifically, staged objectives over modality-specific
expert queries stabilize modality-specific representations and strengthen cross-
modal correspondence before full fusion, while dual query-guided fusion streams
together with logit-level aggregation alleviate fusion-level imbalance by reduc-
ing premature entanglement and hard shared bottlenecks. On CREMA-D and
Kinetics-400, CQFA improves upon state-of-the-art results, achieving 89.94/89.94
and 71.85/71.66 ACC/F1, respectively.
1 INTRODUCTION
Multimodal learning aims to jointly model and integrate information from heterogeneous sources such
as images, audio, text, and video in order to build richer and more robust representations Baltrušaitis
et al. (2019); Caffagni et al. (2024). Multimodal systems are equipped to reflect the complexity of
real-world data by capturing complementary cues across modalities Yin et al. (2024). In recent years,
this paradigm has gained significant traction, fueled by the availability of large-scale paired datasets
and the success of foundation models like CLIP Radford et al. (2021) and CLAP Elizalde et al. (2023).
These advancements have yielded significant performance gains across a range of tasks, including
visual question answering Ding et al. (2022), cross-modal retrieval Wang et al. (2024), audio-visual
processing Du et al. (2025); Wu et al. (2025), and sentiment analysis Das & Singh (2023). Despite
these advances, effective multimodal integration remains challenging. Multimodal fusion may still
exhibit bias or redundancy, and in some cases multimodal models have been observed to perform
worse than strong unimodal baselines, revealing a persistent gap between the promise of multimodal
learning and its realized potential Xu et al. (2025).
To mitigate this issue, existing methods address modality imbalance through interventions at different
stages, including data-level reweighting or modality selection Wei et al. (2024), feedforward modula-
tion and adaptive fusion Zhang et al. (2024a); Wei et al. (2025b); Zhou et al. (2023), and balancing
strategies at the objective or optimization level Wei & Hu (2024); Yang et al. (2024). Despite their
differences, these approaches largely share a common assumption: modality imbalance is primarily
a training-dynamics problem that can be alleviated by reweighting samples, suppressing dominant

This distinction implies that addressing a single level, e.g., improving representation alignment
alone, may not resolve downstream modality underutilization after fusion. This motivates separating
representation stabilization from fusion control in both training and architecture. To address these
coupled sources of imbalance, we propose a framework that explicitly separates representation
stabilization from controlled multimodal fusion. To mitigate representation-level imbalance, CQFA
introduces a multi-stage training scheme over modality-specific expert queries, combining contrastive
alignment, cross-modal matching, and task supervision to stabilize modality-specific representations
and strengthen semantic correspondence before full multimodal fusion. To mitigate fusion-level
imbalance, CQFA employs a shared Q-Former with distinct learnable expert and fusion queries, so
that modality-specific evidence extraction and cross-modal interaction are handled by different query
groups. The final prediction is produced through a decoupled logits-fusion strategy rather than by
compressing both modalities into a single shared fused feature, thereby preserving modality-specific
decision channels and alleviating weak-modality underutilization in the fusion head. Through this
joint design of staged optimization and query-based interaction, CQFA addresses both representation-
level imbalance and fusion-level imbalance within a unified framework, extending prior work that
treats alignment and fusion separately.
To support reproducibility, we will publicly release the implementation, pretrained checkpoints, and
evaluation code upon acceptance. Building on this structural view, the main contributions of this
study are summarized as follows:
(1) We formulate modality imbalance from a two-level structural perspective, distinguishing
representation-level imbalance, reflected by dimension-collapse-induced subspace misalignment
and its associated modality gap, from fusion-level imbalance, reflected by weak-modality underuti-
lization caused by collision-induced interference in shared fusion layers.
(2) We develop a geometric analysis framework for diagnosing modality imbalance, combining
effective-dimension analysis with principal-angle-based modality-gap measurement to quantify
low-dimensional subspace structure, cross-modal misalignment, and their layer-wise evolution.
(3) We propose Cross-Modal Query Fusion and Alignment (CQFA), a query-guided framework that
combines learnable expert and fusion queries, multi-stage alignment objectives, and decoupled logits
fusion to stabilize modality-specific representations and control cross-modal interaction.
(4) Extensive experiments and representation analyses on CREMA-D, A VE, and Kinetics-400 show
that CQFA delivers strong multimodal classification performance and that its gains are associated
with improved high-level alignment and post-fusion consistency, rather than simply minimizing the
raw backbone modality gap.

gradients to reduce modality competition and cross-modal interference Peng et al. (2022); Li et al.
(2023); Fan et al. (2023); Wei et al. (2025a). These studies show that imbalance can be mitigated at

2.2 MODALITYGAP
An emerging body of work has examined multimodal imbalance through the geometry of shared
representation spaces. Liang et al. Liang et al. (2022) first identified themodality gapin multimodal
contrastive learning, formalizing it as a systematic discrepancy between the centers of normalized
embeddings from different modalities within a common embedding space. Subsequent studies refined
this geometric account. In particular, Yi et al. Yi et al. (2025) showed that the modality gap can be
more precisely interpreted through subspace geometry and pairwise alignment, demonstrating that
under subspace constraints the observed center discrepancy is governed by the smallest principal
angle between modality-specific subspaces rather than collapsing to zero. Complementary work has
further investigated why such mismatch persists. Jing et al. Jing et al. (2022) introduceddimensional
collapse, showing that contrastive representations may occupy only a low-dimensional subspace of
the ambient embedding space. Building on this perspective, Zhang et al. Zhang et al. (2024b) related
modality gap in multimodal models to dimensional collapse and quantified the extent of this collapse
through theeffective dimensionderived from the singular spectrum of the representation covariance
matrix. Beyond pre-fusion representation geometry, Chaudhuri et al. Chaudhuri et al. (2025) showed
that modality collapse can also arise during fusion through cross-modal interference in shared fusion
heads, indicating that geometric alignment alone does not guarantee balanced modality utilization.

5 CONCLUSION
In this work, we studied modality imbalance as both representation-level subspace mismatch and
fusion-level weak-modality underuse. We proposed CQFA, a query-guided framework that uses staged
alignment and decoupled logits fusion to improve audio-visual learning. Experiments on CREMA-D
and Kinetics-400 show that CQFA achieves strong performance, and the geometric analyses suggest
that its gains are more closely tied to high-level cross-modal alignment and controlled fusion than to
raw backbone-gap reduction alone.
Limitation.Although CQFA shows strong results on the evaluated benchmarks, our experiments do
not yet include broader comparisons across more audio-visual datasets. Future work will evaluate
CQFA on a wider range of datasets to further verify its generalization ability under different data
distributions and task settings.
